# Source Policy

## Local learning sources

Sanitized local sources define the curriculum and preferred method. Use them before external research.

## Content-only conversion

When converting a PDF, document, note, transcript, or other source to Markdown:

1. Keep learning content only.
2. Remove personal names and identifying personal details.
3. Remove author, collector, uploader, and owner metadata.
4. Remove company, organization, customer, project, hotel, and account names unless a generic category is pedagogically required.
5. Remove email addresses, phone numbers, physical addresses, IDs, usernames, and identifying URLs.
6. Remove headers, footers, page numbers, watermarks, provenance notes, and document metadata.
7. Generalize named examples when the identity is not needed to teach the language.
8. Preserve the meaning, categories, examples, and teaching structure of the source.

## Language usage

When checking modern usage, prefer reputable corpus, dictionary, and editorial guidance. Do not treat a phrase-bank sentence as authoritative merely because it appears in a source.

## Learning science

Prefer university learning centers and peer-reviewed evidence. Favor retrieval practice, spaced practice, interleaving, contextual learning, and productive recall when they fit the lesson.

## Technical sources

Prefer, in order:

1. official platform/framework documentation;
2. official project repositories;
3. mature maintained third-party repositories;
4. general web sources when necessary.

Do not rank technical truth by popularity alone.

# A Hand with Bags and Books

## Situation and goal

You are taking two bags and a box into your home. You can carry the small bag, but you need someone to hold the door. A friend is nearby and can carry one bag briefly. The box can wait. Your friend also has a few books to take upstairs.

Ask for a clear action, accept useful help, respect the friend's time limit, and offer a small favor in return.

{% include scenario-lesson-link.html %}

## Your turn

Say a reply to each friend turn before continuing. Use a hint only when needed. The scripted partner follows a fixed path; it does not understand or grade your reply. For a conversation that adapts to your answers, use the AI prompt below.

{% include roleplay.html %}

For the first turn, ask your friend to hold the door. Keep the model closed until you have tried.

## Change the situation and switch roles {#change-situation}

**Change:** Your friend says, “Sorry, I can't carry anything today, but I can hold the door for a moment.” Accept that smaller offer and explain what you will carry yourself. Try your reply before opening the sample.

**Switch roles:** You are the friend. Offer help with a bag, explain that you cannot move the box, and ask your partner to hold a door for you later. Give one turn, then wait. Respect their answer too.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Sample responses for the change and role reversal</summary>

**Change — sample:** “Yes, please. That would be great. Could you hold the door? I'll carry the bag myself.”

**Switch roles — sample turns:**

1. “Can I help you with that bag?” Wait for the answer.
2. If asked to move the box: “Sorry, I don't have time to move the box.” Wait for the response.
3. Later: “Could you do me a favor and hold the door?” Wait for the answer.
4. If they agree: “Thanks. That would be great.” If they cannot: “No worries. I'll manage.”

These are possible turns, not a script your partner must follow.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Model dialogue — read after your attempt</summary>

**Friend:** Hi! How's unpacking going?  
**You:** Could you do me a favor and hold the door?

**Friend:** Sure. Would you like me to carry something too?  
**You:** Yes, please. That would be great. Could you help me with this small bag?

**Friend:** I can take the bag, but I don't have time to move the box.  
**You:** No worries. I'll ask someone else about the box.

**Friend:** Thanks. I've got some books to take upstairs myself.  
**You:** Can I help you with those books?

**Friend:** Yes, please. You can carry this light stack.  
**You:** Of course. I'll take it upstairs.

</details>

Different natural replies are valid. A successful attempt names the task, responds to the friend's limits, and offers help without deciding the friend's answer for them.

## Practice with an AI partner

Copy this prompt into your preferred AI chat. The website itself does not connect to an AI service.

```text
Play my friend near the entrance to my home. I am the learner, taking two bags and a box inside. I need the door held open and would like help with a small bag. The box can wait. You have a few books to take upstairs yourself.
My level is A2–B1. Use short, natural English and stay in the friend's role.
Give only one short turn, then wait for my reply. Respond to what I actually say.
Let me ask for a specific favor, accept help, respond to a limit, and offer help in return. Do not supply my lines, reveal the full dialogue, or decide what I have agreed to.
Initially you can hold the door and carry one bag briefly, but you cannot move the box. Mention your books after we have discussed my request; leave room for me to offer help.
Introduce one change later: You realize you need to leave sooner and can only hold the door briefly. Let me respond and change my request. If I accept that offer, respond naturally; if I choose another plan, respect it.
If I say "Switch roles", become the person with the bags and let me play the friend. Keep giving only one turn at a time.
If I ask "What should I say next?", give one short hint rather than a complete reply, then wait.
Save corrections until I say "End this session", unless an error blocks meaning.
At the end, use: You said -> Better -> Why -> One reusable example.
Label feedback as Must fix, Natural upgrade, or Optional style. Use my actual replies and do not invent mistakes or successful attempts.
Do not assess listening or pronunciation from text alone.
Start with this one friend turn: "Hi! How's unpacking going?"
```

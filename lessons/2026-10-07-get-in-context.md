### Practice D — Corrections

These are **must-fix** grammar corrections.

| You said | Better | Why | Reusable example |
|---|---|---|---|
| “I get ready for leave.” | “I'm getting ready to leave.” | Use **to + verb** for the action you are preparing to do. The continuous form describes preparation happening now. | “I'm getting ready to start.” |
| “I got to home at six.” | “I got home at six.” | This phrase uses **home** without **to**. | “Did you get home safely?” |
| “I'm getting better at speak.” | “I'm getting better at speaking.” | After **at**, use the **-ing** form for an activity. | “I'm getting better at listening.” |
| “I'll get back you tomorrow.” | “I'll get back to you tomorrow.” | The chunk includes **to** before the person. | “I'll get back to you on Friday.” |

## Core idea

Your friend is waiting outside. You still need your shoes.

> “I'm getting ready. Give me two minutes.”

Later, a colleague asks a question you cannot answer yet.

> “I'll check and get back to you.”

**GET does not have one meaning in every sentence.** Learn the whole phrase and connect it to a situation.

- Preparation → **get ready**
- Arrival → **get home**
- Improvement → **get better**
- Understanding → **get it**
- A later response → **get back to someone**

A **chunk** is a group of words you can remember and use together.

## Useful chunks

| Chunk | Meaning in this situation | Natural example |
|---|---|---|
| **get ready** | Prepare yourself | “I need to get ready for work.” |
| **get home** | Arrive at your home | “I usually get home around six.” |
| **get better** | Improve | “My English is getting better.” |
| **get it** | Understand something | “Oh, I get it now.” |
| **get back to someone** | Contact someone later with a response | “I'll get back to you after lunch.” |

Read each example aloud. Then cover the example column and make your own sentence. Sample responses are in the answer key at the end.

## Real contexts

### Daily life

**Before leaving**

> “Are you ready?”  
> “Not yet. I'm getting ready.”

> “We need to leave soon.”  
> “Okay. I'll get ready now.”

**Arriving home**

> “What time do you get home?”  
> “Around seven.”

> “Are you home yet?”  
> “Yes. I got home ten minutes ago.”

Here, **got** is the past form of **get**.

**Feeling better**

> “How are you feeling?”  
> “I'm getting better, thanks.”

> “Is your cooking improving?”  
> “Yes. I'm getting better at making soup.”

### Work / technical

**Preparing for a meeting**

> “The meeting starts in ten minutes.”  
> “Okay. I'll get ready for it.”

**Understanding an instruction**

> “Save the file before you close it.”  
> “Oh, I get it now.”

**Giving a later answer**

> “Can we finish this by Friday?”  
> “Let me check. I'll get back to you this afternoon.”

**Improving a skill**

> “How is your presentation practice going?”  
> “I'm getting better at explaining my ideas.”

**Your turn:** Choose one daily-life situation and one work situation. Cover the responses and answer aloud in your own words.

## Why these phrases work

The words after **get** help you understand the meaning.

- **Ready** is a state: you move toward being prepared.
- **Home** is a destination: you arrive there.
- **Better** describes improvement.
- **It** refers to the idea you understand.
- **Back to you** suggests returning to the conversation with a response.

These connections are memory aids, not rules for every use of GET.

### Useful contrasts

**“I'm ready” / “I'm getting ready”**

- “I'm ready.” → Preparation is finished.
- “I'm getting ready.” → Preparation is happening.

**“I'm going home” / “I'm getting home”**

- “I'm going home.” → I'm heading toward home.
- “I'm getting home around six.” → Six is my expected arrival time.

**“I'll get back to you” / “I'll be back”**

- “I'll get back to you.” → I'll contact you again.
- “I'll be back.” → I'll return to this place.

**Choose and say the right response:**

1. Your shoes are on, and you can leave immediately.
2. You are leaving the office and heading home.
3. You need to check a price before answering a customer.

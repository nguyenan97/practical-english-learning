## Learn — ask before acting

**Situation:** You are in a shared lounge at a guesthouse. A guesthouse is a small place where travelers stay. A friend is there too. You want to close the window and charge your phone. Your friend has a charger and a spare cable. You also have a pen they can borrow.

**Permission** means someone says you may do something. **Borrow** means use someone else's thing and return it.

{% include chunk-cards.html %}

### Who does the action?

- “Could **you** close the window?” asks the other person to act.
- “Could **I** close the window?” asks permission for you to act.

After **Could I**, use the base verb: “use,” not “using.” After **Is it okay if I**, use a normal sentence: “I use this chair.” Both are useful polite choices. “Can I use this chair?” is also valid; it is not an error.

**Sure, go ahead** gives permission. **Sorry, I'm using it right now** gives a clear reason for saying no. Use that reason only when it is true. Another valid reply is “Sorry, I need it in a minute.” Wait for the answer before acting.

### Use the chunks in different places

- At a café: “Could I move this chair?”
- At work: “Is it okay if I use this desk for ten minutes?”
- When a colleague asks to borrow your spare pen: “Sure, go ahead.”
- When someone asks for the charger connected to your phone: “Sorry, I'm using it right now.”

Look away. Say one changed example for each chunk. Then explain which chunks ask permission, allow an action, or refuse it.

## Practice — ask, wait, adjust

Say your replies aloud. Spend about five minutes on A–D and three minutes on E, including the change and role switch. F is optional for another visit.

### A. Change one detail

Make two versions of each frame. Use a different object or action each time.

1. “Could I ______, please?”
2. “Is it okay if I ______?”

### B. Recall from situations

Look away from the examples.

1. You feel cold. Ask permission to close the window yourself.
2. You want to use a spare chair. Check that this is okay.
3. A friend asks to borrow your spare pen. You agree.
4. A friend asks for your charger. It is charging your phone, so say no and explain.

### C. Ask and answer

Your partner asks, “Could I use your charger?” You are using it now. Refuse politely.

They then ask, “Is it okay if I use your spare cable?” The cable is available. Give permission.

Now swap roles. Ask those two questions yourself and wait for each answer. If the charger is unavailable, ask about the cable; do not assume permission.

### D. Fix an error or choose a style

1. **Must fix:** “Could I using your pen?”
2. **Must fix:** “Is it okay if I to sit here?”
3. **Optional style:** “Can I use your charger?” Say a polite alternative. The original is already valid.

### E. Try a connected conversation

Use {% include scenario-link.html label="the shared-lounge scenario" %}. Answer the five friend turns before opening the model.

**Change:** The spare cable does not fit your phone. You have your own charger in your bag. Ask permission to use the socket by the door with your own charger. A socket is the place where you plug in a charger.

**Switch roles:** Be the friend. Give permission to close the window, refuse use of your charger because you are using it, and offer your spare cable. Ask to borrow the learner's pen. Wait after each turn.

### F. Make it yours — optional

Choose a real or imaginary shared space. Ask permission to use something. Imagine a refusal, then ask about another option. Do not include private details.

## Exit task — a new place, no notes

You are preparing for a short meeting at work. A colleague has a spare chair, but they are using their desk. An empty table is nearby. You have a spare pen they may borrow.

Cover the examples. Speak one turn at a time:

1. Ask permission to move the spare chair to the empty table.
2. Your colleague says, “Sure. But please leave my desk free.” Ask permission to use the empty table instead. Wait for an answer before using it.
3. They say, “Yes, that's fine. Could I borrow your spare pen?” Give permission.
4. A second colleague asks, “Could I use your phone?” You are using it to check the meeting time. Refuse politely and explain.

Use all four target chunks across your replies if they fit. Other natural wording is valid too; note which target chunks you could recall independently.

### Delayed recall

Describe your next meal for about one minute. Then recall the four chunks without notes: two ways to ask permission, one way to give it, and one way to refuse because you are using the item.

### Check your attempt

- Did you make clear who would do the action?
- Did each request name the action or object?
- Did you respect the limit and ask about another option?
- Did you give or refuse permission for the right reason?
- Which chunks could you say without a model?

Retry a difficult reply with a new object before saving your self-assessment.

**Remember:** Ask first. Wait for the answer. Respect a no. Ask about another option.

## Answer key and sample responses

All open responses are samples, not the only correct answers. Check the action, object, and reason against the task. Use the criteria below to check your own wording.

### Review — samples

1. “Could you help me with this heavy bag, please?” Changed object: “Could you help me with this box, please?”
2. “No worries. I'll ask someone else.”

**Check:** Ask for help with a named object; respect the refusal without pressure.

### Learn — changed examples and purposes

- “Could I use your pen, please?” asks permission for your action.
- “Is it okay if I open the window?” checks whether your action is acceptable.
- “Sure, go ahead” lets someone do what they asked.
- “Sorry, I'm using it right now” refuses because the thing is in use.

**Check:** Your changed examples keep the same purpose. The refusal must fit an item actually in use.

### Practice A — two versions

1. “Could I borrow your pen, please?” / “Could I close the window, please?”
2. “Is it okay if I use this chair?” / “Is it okay if I sit here?”

**Check:** Use a base verb after “Could I” and a complete clause after “if.” A clause here means words with a person and an action, such as “I sit here.”

### Practice B — samples

1. “Could I close the window, please?”
2. “Is it okay if I use this chair?”
3. “Sure, go ahead.”
4. “Sorry, I'm using it right now.”

**Check:** Ask before acting; give permission only for the spare item; explain why the charger is unavailable.

### Practice C — samples for both roles

**Partner:** Could I use your charger?  
**You:** Sorry, I'm using it right now.

**Partner:** Is it okay if I use your spare cable?  
**You:** Sure, go ahead.

**Swapped roles:** Ask “Could I use your charger?” and wait. If it is unavailable, ask “Is it okay if I use your spare cable?” Wait again. If they agree, say “Thanks.” If they refuse, say “That's okay. I'll ask someone else.”

**Check:** Answer the actual request. When asking, wait for each decision and respect it.

### Practice D — corrections and style

| Type | You said | Better | Why | One reusable example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Must fix | Could I using your pen? | Could I use your pen? | Use the base verb after could I. | Could I borrow this book? |
| Must fix | Is it okay if I to sit here? | Is it okay if I sit here? | The clause after if needs a verb without to here. | Is it okay if I use this desk? |
| Optional style | Can I use your charger? | Could I use your charger, please? | Both are valid. This is another polite choice. | Could I use your cable, please? |

### Practice E — model, change, and role switch

The {% include scenario-link.html label="scenario" %} contains the ten-turn model and role-switch samples.

**Change — sample:** “The cable doesn't fit my phone. Is it okay if I use the socket by the door with my own charger?” Wait. After permission: “Thanks. I'll use my own charger.” If refused: “That's okay. I'll charge it later.”

**Role switch — sample turns:** After a window request: “Sure, go ahead.” After a charger request: “Sorry, I'm using it right now. You can use my spare cable.” After a cable request: “Sure, go ahead. Could I borrow your pen?” If they say no, accept the answer.

**Check:** Request an available alternative without claiming permission. In the other role, respond to the request your partner actually makes.

### Practice F — sample

“Could I use this desk for ten minutes?” After “Sorry, I need it now”: “Is it okay if I use the table over there instead?” Wait for an answer.

**Check:** Name a shared space, ask about a different option after the refusal, and wait.

### Exit task — samples

1. “Could I move your spare chair to the empty table, please?”
2. “Is it okay if I use the empty table instead?”
3. “Sure, go ahead.”
4. “Sorry, I'm using it right now. I'm checking the meeting time.”

**Check:** Move the chair only after permission. Keep the colleague's desk free. Give permission for the spare pen and explain the phone refusal. Note any hints you needed.

### Delayed recall — samples

**Meal description:** “I'll have rice and vegetables for dinner. I might make some soup too.” Your own meal plan is valid.

**Four chunks:** Could I ...? / Is it okay if I ...? / Sure, go ahead. / Sorry, I'm using it right now.

**Check:** Recall the complete openings and match them to the four purposes without reading. Reading this key is preparation for a fresh attempt, not evidence of mastery.

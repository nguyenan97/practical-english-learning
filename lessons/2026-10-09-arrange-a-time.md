## Learn — make a plan together

**Situation:** You and a friend want to meet for coffee on Saturday. You know the day, but you need a time and place that suit both of you.

A useful sequence is: ask about availability → suggest → adjust → confirm.

{% include chunk-cards.html %}

### Daily life and work

- Coffee with a friend: “Are you free on Saturday?”
- Choosing a place: “How about the café near the station?”
- Scheduling a call: “Ten thirty? That works for me.”
- Changing a meeting: “I can't make it at ten. How about eleven?”

### Why it works and contrast

**How about ...?** offers a choice. Use a noun (“How about three?”), an -ing form (“How about meeting at three?”), or a full clause (“How about we meet at three?”). **That works for me** accepts the plan. **I can't make it** says you cannot attend, and another option keeps the conversation moving.

“I don't want to meet” means you do not want the meeting. “I can't make it at three” says the time does not work; you may still want to meet.

Say the chunks aloud, then explain each purpose without copying the table.

## Practice — build a workable plan

Spend about five minutes on A–D and three minutes on the conversation in E. Try F on another visit if you need more time.

### A. Change one detail

Make two versions of each frame:

1. “Are you free ______?”
2. “How about ______?”
3. “I can't make it at ______. How about ______?”

### B. Retrieve from situations

1. Ask a friend whether they are available tomorrow afternoon.
2. Suggest meeting at four.
3. Accept four because it suits your schedule.
4. Decline three and suggest half past three.

### C. Reply and ask a follow-up

Your friend says: “I'm free after lunch.”

Suggest a time. After they agree, ask where you should meet.

### D. Make it natural

Correct these sentences:

1. “Are you free in Saturday?”
2. “How about meet at three?”
3. “That work for me.”

### E. Try the whole conversation

Use {% include scenario-link.html label="the coffee-planning scenario" %}. Answer the friend-only turns without reading the model.

**Changed situation:** Your friend cannot meet on Saturday. Suggest Sunday and ask about a time.

**Switch roles:** Invite your partner, decline their first time politely, and agree on an alternative.

### F. Make it yours

Plan a real or imaginary call. Say three sentences: ask about availability, suggest a time, and give an alternative if needed.

## Exit task — transfer to a new situation

A colleague wants a short call tomorrow at ten. You are available at eleven or after two.

Without notes:

1. Decline ten politely.
2. Suggest eleven.
3. If eleven does not work, suggest a time after two.
4. Confirm the final time after they agree.

Use at least three target chunks across your turns. Do not invent agreement before the other person responds.

### Delayed recall and success criteria

Describe your plans for tomorrow for one minute. Then recall the four chunks.

You succeeded if you offered a workable alternative, responded to your partner, and finished with a clear time. If you copied the model, try again with new times before recording a score.

## Answer key and sample responses

Different times and natural responses are valid if they achieve the goal.

### Review

1. “Could you say the meeting time again, please?”

### Learn — explain the chunks

- “Are you free ...?” asks if someone has time.
- “How about ...?” suggests an option.
- “That works for me” accepts a suitable plan.
- “I can't make it at ...” says a time is not possible for you.

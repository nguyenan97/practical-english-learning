## Learn — keep the conversation moving

**Situation:** A colleague gives you a deadline during a call. The connection is unclear, and you need to know the right day and time before sending a report.

You do not have to pretend you understand. Ask again, then confirm the detail.

{% include chunk-cards.html %}

### Daily life and work

- At a café: “Could you say that again, please? I missed the price.”
- During directions: “Could you speak a little more slowly?”
- Planning with a friend: “Do you mean this Saturday?”
- On a work call: “Just to check, you need the report by five, right?”

### Why it works

**Could you ...?** makes a polite request. **Do you mean ...?** proposes one possible meaning. **Just to check, ...** confirms a detail you think you already know.

**Contrast:** “I don't understand” states the problem. “Could you say that again, please?” gives the other person a clear next action. Repetition may not explain a difficult idea; in that case, ask what the idea means.

Say each chunk once, then cover it and explain its purpose in simple English.

## Practice — ask, confirm, respond

### A. Change one detail

Make two versions of each frame:

1. “Could you say ______ again, please?”
2. “Do you mean ______?”
3. “Just to check, ______, right?”

### B. Retrieve from situations

Say one sentence for each situation without looking at the chunks:

1. You missed the last part of a sentence.
2. Someone gives directions too quickly.
3. A colleague says “next Friday,” but you are unsure which date they mean.
4. You think the call starts at ten. Confirm it.

### C. Reply and ask a follow-up

Your partner says: “We need two copies before lunch.”

Check the number of copies, then ask where to put them. Do not ask two unrelated questions at once.

### D. Make it natural

Correct these sentences:

1. “Could you say again that?”
2. “Could you speak more slow?”
3. “Do you means this Friday?”

### E. Practice a real call

Open [the unclear-deadline scenario]({{ '/scenarios/check-understanding.html' | relative_url }}). Try the colleague-only turns before reading the model dialogue.

**Changed situation:** The repeated deadline is Thursday, not Friday. Confirm the corrected day.

**Switch roles:** Give your partner a deadline, repeat it when asked, and correct one mistaken detail politely.

### F. Make it yours

Think of a safe, everyday situation where you need clarification. Say a two-sentence response using one request and one confirmation. Do not include private names or account details.

## Exit task — a new situation, no notes

A receptionist tells you that an appointment is at fourteen thirty on the second floor. You missed the time and are unsure whether you need to arrive early.

1. Ask for the time again.
2. Check that it means 2:30 p.m.
3. Confirm the floor.
4. Ask whether you should arrive early.

Use at least two target chunks naturally. This task is about choosing language; reading the prompt does not test listening ability.

### Delayed recall and success criteria

Talk about your morning for one minute. Then recall all four chunks without notes.

You succeeded if you asked clearly, confirmed the important details, and did not simply copy the model. If you needed a hint, record that honestly and try a different detail.

## Answer key and sample responses

Open-ended responses can differ. These examples show natural ways to achieve the goal.

### Review

1. “Oh, I get it now.”
2. “I'll check and get back to you after lunch.”

### Learn — explain the chunks

- “Could you say that again, please?” asks the person to repeat.
- “Could you speak a little more slowly?” asks them to reduce their speed.
- “Do you mean ...?” checks a possible meaning.
- “Just to check, ...” confirms a detail before you act.

### Practice A

1. “Could you say the time again, please?” / “Could you say the last part again, please?”
2. “Do you mean this Saturday?” / “Do you mean two copies?”
3. “Just to check, we start at ten, right?” / “Just to check, you need the report today, right?”

### Practice B

### Practice D — must fix

| You said | Better | Why | Reusable example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Could you say again that? | Could you say that again, please? | Put that after say, before again. | Could you say the time again? |
| Could you speak more slow? | Could you speak more slowly? | Slowly describes the action. | Could you read it more slowly? |
| Do you means this Friday? | Do you mean this Friday? | Use the base verb after do. | Do you mean this afternoon? |
